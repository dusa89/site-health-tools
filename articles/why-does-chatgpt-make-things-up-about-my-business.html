Why does ChatGPT make things up about my business?

A customer asks about your senior discount. You never offered one — ChatGPT invented it. Why AI chatbots fabricate facts about real businesses, and the 4 moves that give them less empty space to fill.

A customer calls and asks about your senior discount. You do not have a senior discount. You never have. But ChatGPT told them you do — confidently, with details — and now you are either honoring a discount you never offered or explaining to a confused customer that the robot lied. This happens to real businesses every week. Here is the verdict up front: AI chatbots invent facts about businesses as a core behavior, not a rare glitch — and the only fix is feeding them better raw material everywhere they look.

This is not a theory. In June 2023, lawyers in a real federal case (Mata v. Avianca) filed a brief citing half a dozen court cases that did not exist — ChatGPT had invented them, complete with fake judges and fake quotes, and the lawyers never checked. It was widely reported and the lawyers were sanctioned. If a chatbot will fabricate case law for attorneys, it will absolutely fabricate your prices, your hours, and a "family discount" you never dreamed of. The mechanism is the same in both cases.

Why it invents instead of saying "I don't know"

A chatbot does not look up your business the way you imagine. It predicts the most plausible-sounding next words, trained on the whole internet. When a customer asks "does Acme Plumbing offer free estimates?", the model reaches for what it knows about you — and if what it knows is thin, it fills the gaps with what sounds right for a business like yours. Most plumbers offer free estimates, so it says you do. It is not lying; it has no concept of truth. It is guessing in fluent English, and fluent guesses are the hardest kind to catch.

Where it gets its raw material (and where the gaps are)

The model builds its picture of you from the same public sources everything else uses: your website, your Google Business Profile, review sites, and directory listings. The fabrication problem is really a thin-data problem. A business with a detailed website, a complete profile, and 200 reviews naming real services gives the model a rich picture to draw from — less guessing needed. A business with a five-page website, an empty profile, and 6 reviews gives it almost nothing, so it improvises. That is also why being invisible to AI and losing to a competitor in AI answers are the same disease at different stages: thin public data, different symptoms.

Why you cannot just correct it

There is no edit button on a chatbot. Every answer is generated fresh, so correcting one chat changes nothing about the next one — and the next customer asks a slightly different question anyway. The businesses that get this right stopped trying to fix the answers and started fixing the inputs: every public source, made richer and more consistent, so there is less empty space for the model to fill with invention.

What to do about it

Four moves, in order of impact. First, put real facts on your website in plain text. Your actual services, your actual prices or price ranges, your actual hours — as regular text on the page. Not in a PDF menu, not baked into an image, not hidden behind "call for pricing." If a human has to call to learn it, the AI will invent it instead. Second, complete your Google Business Profile — every field, accurate, kept current. It is one of the heaviest-weighted sources these models draw on. Third, get reviews that name real services ("they replaced our water heater same-day") rather than generic five-star noise; reviews are one of the few sources that describe what you do in natural language, which is exactly what the models ingest (we wrote a whole guide on the reviews-to-AI pipeline). Fourth, keep the same facts everywhere — directories, social profiles, your site. Contradictory sources do not cancel out; they teach the model that facts about you are negotiable.

One honest caveat to close on: this shrinks fabrication dramatically, but nobody can promise it reaches zero — the models will always guess a little. Anyone selling you a "guaranteed accurate AI profile" is selling the one thing in this space that does not exist. What you can buy is knowing exactly what is being said right now, and a standing watch over it. Which is check #1 of 40.
