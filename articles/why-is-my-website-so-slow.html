Why is my website so slow?

You opened your own website on your phone, counted the seconds, and felt your stomach drop. Here is what that slowness actually is, why it costs you customers, and how to measure it without hiring a developer.

When someone says a website is “slow,” they mean one specific thing: the time between tapping the link and the moment the page’s main content — the headline, the photo, the reason they came — actually appears. Google calls this measurement Largest Contentful Paint. You don’t need the jargon; you need the number, in seconds.

How slow is too slow?

Google’s own bar is public: under 2.5 seconds is good, 2.5 to 4 seconds needs work, and over 4 seconds is poor. And the stakes are real — Google’s published research found that more than half of mobile visitors leave a page that takes longer than 3 seconds to load. They don’t complain. They don’t try again later. They tap back and call whoever loaded faster.

The four usual culprits

Photos that are far bigger than they need to be. This is the number-one cause on small-business sites. A phone screen never needs a 5-megabyte image, but cameras and stock-photo sites hand you exactly that, and most website builders upload it as-is.

Too many files loading before the page can show. Every plugin, tracker, chat widget, and font adds a file your visitor’s phone must download and run before anything appears. Each one is small; together they are a traffic jam.

Autoplay video. A background video looks expensive and loads like it too — often the single heaviest thing on the page, forced on every visitor whether they watch it or not.

Cheap shared hosting. If the server itself takes two seconds just to answer, no amount of image-shrinking fixes it. The server’s response time is the floor your speed can never go below.
