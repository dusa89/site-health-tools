Why doesn't Google's AI ever mention my business?

You Googled your own trade plus your town, and above the familiar list of websites sat a new box — Google's AI answer, written in full sentences, naming three businesses. None of them was yours. You rank on page one of Google. In that moment, it did not matter at all.

That box has a name: AI Overviews. Google launched it in 2024, and it has grown fast. In June 2026, an AI Overview appeared on 39.4 percent of U.S. desktop Google searches — up from 25.8 percent a year earlier (Comscore's Q2 2026 AI Intelligence Report, reported by Search Engine Land, September 2026). A separate BrightEdge analysis from February 2026 put it at nearly half of the queries it tracks. For a growing share of customers, the AI's answer is the search result. If your business is never in it, you are losing conversations you will never know happened.

Ranking on Google does not get you into the answer

This is the part that stings. You would assume the AI just quotes the websites that already rank. It mostly does not. BrightEdge found that only about 17 percent of the sources cited in AI Overviews also appear in Google's organic top 10. The AI is reading a different web than the one you optimized for.

The gap is even wider for local businesses. BrightLocal's 2026 study — 60,970 localized checks across 1,355 business locations — found the businesses it tracked appeared in Google Maps about 66 percent of the time, but were named in AI Overviews only 38 percent of the time. And there is no single “AI ranking” to chase: Google's own AI Overviews and its AI Mode shared only about 29 percent of the businesses they named, and ChatGPT overlapped with Google's AI products by roughly 19 to 20 percent. Each product builds its own shortlist.

What the AI is actually reading about you

So where does the shortlist come from? BrightLocal dug into the sources: for Google's own AI products, the single most-used source is your Google Business Profile — Google used it for virtually every location. Yelp is second for AI Overviews and the top source for ChatGPT. In total, 415 directories showed up as sources, and about 93 percent of the unique source domains were business websites — which generated roughly 42 percent of all citations. AI assistants also read your reviews — from Yelp, your Business Profile, and elsewhere — to gather the details they use to describe you.

In plain terms: the AI builds its answer from what it can verify about you across the web, and it trusts Google's own listing about you above almost everything else.

Four free fixes, in order of leverage

1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile. Real category, real services, real photos, real hours — and reply to every review, because those replies become more text attached to your name that the AI can read. This is the AI's favorite source about you. Cost: zero.

2. Put a quotable answer at the top of each service page. Write a direct, 40-to-60-word answer to the question customers actually ask — “who fixes water heaters in North Las Vegas?” — right at the top of the page, in plain sentences with your trade and your town named. Add FAQ-style questions and answers to the page too. You are writing for the AI's highlighter, not a poetry contest.

3. Make your name, address, and phone identical everywhere. Your website, Yelp, Facebook, Angi, every directory — same spelling, same punctuation. Hundreds of directories feed these systems, and conflicting listings make the AI unsure you are one business at all. Free, boring, and disproportionately effective.

4. Do not block the AI crawlers. Check your website's robots.txt file — some sites accidentally block the crawlers that AI products use to read the web. If the AI cannot read your page, it cannot cite it.

The honest check you can run today

Search the way a customer would — your trade, your town, in your own words — and read the AI answer at the top. Try longer questions too: one study found AI summaries appear on only about 8 percent of one- or two-word searches but 53 percent of searches with ten or more words (Pew Research). Note who gets named and what gets cited, then repeat in a few months. These systems update slowly, so think in months, not days — and every fix above also helps your regular Google ranking, so none of the work is wasted either way.
