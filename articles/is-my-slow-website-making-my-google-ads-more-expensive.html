Is My Slow Website Making My Google Ads More Expensive?

Every click on your Google ad costs you real money. If the page that click lands on is slow, you get hit twice: Google can charge you more for each click, and more of the visitors you paid for leave before they see a thing.

All facts below are reported from the cited sources, checked 2026-10-13 - not promises about your situation. Google does not publish exactly how its scores are weighted; anyone who quotes you an exact dollar saving from a faster page is estimating, not measuring.

The short answer

Very likely, yes - through Google's Quality Score system. Google grades every keyword you bid on with a 1-to-10 score, and one of its three parts is the landing page experience: whether the page your ad leads to is relevant, trustworthy, fast, and mobile-friendly. A slow landing page drags that score down, and a low score means you pay more per click for the same ad position than a competitor with a fast, relevant page. Nobody at Google will tell you the exact price difference - the formula is theirs - but the direction is not disputed anywhere in the industry: better landing pages pay less per click.

What Quality Score actually is

Quality Score is Google's 1-to-10 rating of each keyword in your account. It has three parts: how often people click your ad (expected click-through rate), how closely your ad matches what the person searched (ad relevance), and the experience of the page the ad leads to (landing page experience). Your score affects two things you care about: where your ad shows up, and what you actually pay. Two advertisers can bid the same amount for the same keyword, and the one with the higher Quality Score gets the better position and pays less per click. Google's own documentation states this plainly: advertisers with higher scores pay less for the same ad position.

Why speed is baked into the landing page score

Landing page experience includes page speed directly, along with mobile-friendliness and trust signals. Google watches what real visitors do on your page: if people click your ad and leave within seconds, that signals a bad experience, and a slow page is one of the most common reasons for it. Most ad clicks now come from phones, so Google evaluates the mobile version of your page. This is a different mechanism from the organic-ranking question we covered separately - there, speed is a small tiebreaker between competing pages; here, speed feeds a score that moves your actual price per click.

The two ways a slow page burns your ad budget

First, you pay more for every click. A poor landing page experience can drop your Quality Score by a few points, and every point down costs you more per click for the same position. Second, you pay for clicks that produce nothing: visitors who bounce before the page finishes loading cost you the click fee and never see your offer. Google's long-standing published benchmark says more than half of mobile visits are abandoned after three seconds - those are clicks you bought and handed straight back. The two effects compound against you: you pay more per click and convert fewer of them. That compounding effect is our analysis, built from the sources below.

The one number to aim for

The consistent guidance across paid-search practitioners is simple: your landing page should load in under two seconds on a phone. That is not a Google-published requirement - it is the industry working target. Google's own published benchmark of abandonment after three seconds is the outer bound: three seconds is where you start losing the majority, not where you are safe. And measure the pages that take paid traffic, not your homepage: many businesses send every ad to the homepage, which is heavier, slower, and less relevant than a page built for the ad - costing you on Quality Score twice over.

How to check whether speed is costing you right now

Open your Google Ads account, go to the Keywords view, and add the Quality Score columns - Google offers Quality Score, Landing page experience, Expected CTR, and Ad relevance as columns you can add. Look at the Landing page experience column for the keywords that matter to you: "Below average" or "Average" on your money keywords is the flag. Then run those exact landing-page URLs through a speed check and note the seconds figure on mobile. If the flag and a slow number line up on the same page, you have found a page where speed is very likely inflating what you pay - and where visitors are bouncing before they convert.

The fix list that matters for ad landing pages

The standard speed fixes apply, but ad landing pages get two special rules. First, do not send paid traffic to your homepage: a dedicated page that matches the ad's promise loads faster because it carries less weight, converts better because it has one job, and scores better on relevance. Second, strip the landing page of anything that does not earn the click: the chat widget, the review carousel, the background video, the pop-up that fires on entry - every one of these slows the page and each one is a distraction from the single button the visitor came to press. One page, one offer, one button is the shape that both visitors and Google reward.
