What does one extra second of load time actually cost your business?

Nobody spends seconds — you spend customers. The research on what a slow website costs is better than you might expect, and the math for a one-person business is simple enough to do on a napkin.

Every guide about website speed talks in seconds. Owners do not spend seconds — they spend customers. So here is the question translated into the currency you actually care about: if your website takes one second longer to load, how many visitors leave, how many never call, and what does that cost you? The research on this is better than you might expect — and more honest than the headlines make it sound.

The headline number: 53% gone at three seconds

Google published this one back in January 2017, on its own AdSense blog, and the industry still quotes it because nothing has contradicted it: 53% of mobile site visits are abandoned if the page takes longer than 3 seconds to load. That was DoubleClick’s research, and it holds up as the industry reference. A follow-up Google benchmark study found the cost climbs fast — as page load time goes from 1 second to 7 seconds, the likelihood of a visitor abandoning the page rises 113%. Read that again: the same visitor, the same page, just six seconds slower — and abandoning more than doubles.

The tenth-of-a-second money finding

The study that changed how the industry talks about speed came from Deloitte and Google in March 2020, titled “Milliseconds Make Millions.” Over four weeks they measured mobile sites across retail, travel, luxury, and lead-generation brands in Europe and the US, isolating speed from everything else. Their finding: a mere 0.1-second improvement in mobile load time raised retail conversions 8.4% and average order value 9.2%; travel conversions rose 10.1%. On lead-generation pages — the category closest to a plumber’s “call us” page — the bounce rate improved 8.3%.

Think about what that means in reverse. A tenth of a second — the blink-and-miss gap between “fast” and “slightly less fast” — was worth nearly a tenth of a retail site’s conversions. Nobody is claiming your plumbing site gains exactly 8.4% from shaving 0.1 seconds. The point is finer than that: speed moves customer behavior at a scale of seconds and tenths of seconds that no owner would guess by feel.

The honest caveats

Both big studies lean toward ecommerce and travel — sites where a visitor can buy in two clicks. Your business probably earns its money one phone call or one quote form at a time, and the studies’ exact percentages will not map onto your books one-to-one. Also note the dates: 2017 and 2020. Human impatience has not gotten better since then, and the average mobile landing page still takes around 22 seconds to fully load (Google’s 2017 benchmark figure, still widely cited) against an expectation of under 3. Treat the numbers as the direction and scale of the effect, not as a promise about your site.

The illustration: one slower second, one month

Say 200 people a month find your site — a modest local-services number. Say your homepage takes 5 seconds to appear on a phone, where Google’s good band ends at 2.5. The research above says a meaningful share of those 200 never wait to see what you offer: they hit back, and the person they called instead loaded in 2 seconds. If your normal close rate on website visitors is 10%, losing even a fifth of the patient remainder to impatience costs you 4 jobs a month. At a few hundred dollars a job — and that is illustration math, not a measured figure for your business — one extra second of load time is a four-figure line item on your monthly ledger, and nobody sends you a bill for it. It just quietly never arrives.
