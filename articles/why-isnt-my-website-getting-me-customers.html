Why isn’t my website getting me any customers?

You paid for the website. Customers tell you they saw it. And yet the phone rings from referrals, from the truck, from word of mouth — never from the website. The site is not broken. It is leaking.

Most owners ask this question as one question: “Is anyone even visiting my site?” That is the wrong first question, and answering it can waste a year. Visitors are only half the job. The half that pays is what happens after they arrive — and that is where most small business websites quietly fail. A site can get steady traffic and still produce zero jobs, because every visitor hits one of the same leaks on the way in and leaves without calling.

The five leaks, in order of leverage

1. It is too slow on phones. This is the single biggest customer-killer on the list, and it is invisible to you because you always look at your site on your office computer or home wifi. Google’s published benchmark found that more than half of mobile visitors leave a page that takes longer than 3 seconds to load — and Google’s own pass/fail line (the Core Web Vitals “good” threshold) is 2.5 seconds for the main content to appear. Most visitors who leave a slow site do not tell you. They just hire the competitor whose site loaded.

2. There is no clear next step. A visitor who decides to call you should be able to do it in one tap. We see sites where the phone number is buried three menus deep, where the only contact is a form asking for ten fields, where the “call” button is an image that is not clickable on a phone. Every extra step between “I want to hire these people” and “I reached them” costs you a share of the people who were ready. The fix is boring and free: a tap-to-call button at the top of every page, and a form that asks for name, phone, and what they need — nothing else.

3. It never answers the two questions. A customer visiting your site has two questions and almost no patience: how much will this cost me and can I trust these people. Sites that dodge the first one with “call for a quote” everywhere lose the comparison shoppers — you do not need exact prices, but a ballpark or a starting-at figure keeps people reading. Sites that dodge the second one — stock photos instead of your crew, no reviews on the page, no real address — lose everyone else. Put three genuine reviews and one photo of real work on the homepage. It does more than a redesign.

4. It is broken on the phone in a way nobody reports. Not “slow” — broken. The menu button that does nothing. Text so small it cannot be read without pinching. Tap targets packed so tight customers hit the wrong one. The contact form that looks fine on a monitor and overflows the screen on a phone. You will never get a complaint about this; customers just leave. The test costs nothing: open your own site on your phone, on cellular data, and try to do the one thing a customer would do.

5. The doors open onto empty rooms. Dead links, gallery pages that 404, “services” pages from 2019, and the quietest killer of all — a contact form that submits, says “thank you,” and sends the message nowhere because the notification email changed two years ago. Every one of those is a customer who tried to reach you and concluded you were gone. Our guide to dead links shows how to find the broken doors for free.

The honest check you can run today

Do what a customer does, not what an owner does. Put your phone on cellular — not your wifi — and open your homepage. Count the seconds out loud until you can read the main headline; past 3 and you have leak number one. Then try to call yourself from the site: if it takes more than two taps, you have leak number two. Then fill out your own contact form and confirm the message actually arrives somewhere you check. That ten-minute walk is the cheapest audit in business — it is exactly what a frustrated visitor experiences, and you will feel every leak yourself.
