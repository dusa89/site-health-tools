What are SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, in plain English?

Think of them as three stamps on every email you send. SPF is a public list, kept in your domain's DNS records, naming which mail servers are allowed to send email for you. When Gmail gets a message claiming to be from your business, it checks the list. DKIM is a digital signature your mail server adds to each message — like a wax seal that proves the email wasn't altered in transit. DMARC is your written instruction to inbox providers: here's what to do with mail that fails the first two checks — deliver it, quarantine it, or reject it — and where to send you reports about it.

When any of the three are missing, two bad things happen quietly. First, your real email looks more suspicious, so more of it lands in spam — the quotes and invoices your customers never see. Second, anyone can send email pretending to be your domain, and inbox providers have no instruction to stop it.

The fix is three small entries in your domain's DNS settings — the same place your website's address lives, usually your registrar like GoDaddy or Cloudflare.
