Why does my competitor show up in the Google Maps 3-pack and I don't?

Search your trade plus your town and the same three businesses own the top of the page — a map, phone numbers, star ratings, “open now.” You are somewhere below the fold, or on the map at all. It is not luck, and it is not ad spend. Here is what the 2026 survey of 47 local-search experts says actually decides those three spots.

That block of three is called the local pack, and it is the single most valuable free placement in local search: three names, a map, review counts, and a call button, sitting above everything else. Losing it means losing the customers who never scroll. The good news is that Google has said out loud what the pack is graded on — relevance, distance, and prominence — and the industry’s annual survey of local-search experts has ranked the individual signals underneath. The bad news: about half of the decision comes from two things most owners have never maintained.

What 47 experts say decides the pack

Whitespark’s 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey had 47 local-search practitioners score 187 individual signals for their impact on the map pack. The top five, in order: the primary Google Business Profile category (ranked #1), the physical proximity of the business to the searcher (#2), keywords in the GBP business title (#3), the physical address being in the city of the search (#4), and the business being open at the time of the search (#5). Then reviews: high numerical ratings at #6, quantity of native Google reviews with text at #9, review recency at #11, and a sustained influx of reviews over time — not bursts — at #14.

Grouped another way, the expert consensus puts the weight at roughly 32 percent Google Business Profile signals, 20 percent review signals, 15 percent on-page website signals, 9 percent behavioral signals like clicks and direction requests, 8 percent links, 6 percent citations, 6 percent personalization, and 4 percent social. Your profile plus your reviews is about half the game. Your website — the thing you probably paid the most for — is 15 percent of this particular contest. That is not a criticism of websites; it is just the scoreboard for this specific placement.

The levers you can actually move

1. Your primary category — the #1 single factor. “Electrician” versus “Electrical contractor” versus “Handyman” changes which searches you get named for. Most businesses set this once, years ago, and never look at it again. Pick the category your customers actually search, then add accurate secondary categories underneath. This is free, takes 10 minutes, and it outranks everything else on the list.

2. Reviews, run as a system, not a favor. This is where the gap usually is. BrightLocal’s 2026 local consumer survey (1,002 US adults) found 97 percent of consumers read reviews for local businesses, 68 percent require a minimum of four stars (up from 55 percent a year earlier), and 31 percent will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher — nearly double the prior year’s 17 percent. On volume: 47 percent of consumers say they will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews at all, and in competitive verticals the businesses actually sitting in the pack have a median of 50 or more reviews. But recency is the sharp edge — roughly 3 in 4 consumers only care about reviews written in the last 3 months, and the experts rank sustained weekly flow above a big stale total. Five new reviews a month, every month, beats 50 reviews from 2 years ago. And reply: 42 percent of consumers say they are unlikely to use a business that never responds to reviews.

3. A complete profile. Google’s own guidance is blunt: local results are based primarily on relevance, distance, and prominence, complete and accurate profiles are more likely to show up, and there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Every field you leave blank is a relevance question Google answers for you. Hours matter more than owners think — being open at the time of the search is the #5 factor, and wrong holiday hours quietly demote you during your own busiest days. Photos are legitimacy signals: a profile with recent job photos reads as alive; a profile frozen since 2023 reads as a rumor of a business.

4. Consistency everywhere else. Your name, address, and phone need to match on your website, Yelp, directories, and social — same spelling, same punctuation. Conflicting listings make Google unsure you are one business at all. The website behind the profile counts too: domain authority, local landing pages, and real service pages feed the prominence side of the score. If your site has technical errors or no pages for what you actually do, the profile carries less weight than it could.

The one lever you cannot move

Distance. Proximity of your address to the searcher is the #2 factor, and you cannot optimize your way around physics. A competitor 4 miles closer to the customer’s corner will beat you for that customer’s search no matter how good your profile is — and a service-area business that hides its address relies on declared service areas for that distance signal, which makes completeness and prominence even more important to get right. This is why the pack looks different at every point on the map: a business can rank #1 from its own zip code and fall to #15 in a suburb 10 miles out that is still inside its service area. Search from your own office and you will always see a rosier picture than the customer does. Measure from their corner, not yours.

What will not get you there

Paying Google — the company says it outright, there is no purchased slot in the pack. Keyword-stuffing your business name (“Mike’s Plumbing — Best Plumber in Las Vegas 24/7 Cheap”) is against policy and can get the whole profile suspended. Fake reviews are now both a Google suspension risk and an FTC Consumer Review Rule violation carrying civil penalties up to 53,088 dollars per violation. And the single burst — 50 reviews begged at once, then silence for a year — loses to a steady weekly drip every time. None of this is a trick, which is exactly why it works: it compounds.

The honest check you can run today

Open a private browsing window, set the location to your customer’s side of town, and search your trade plus the town. Screenshot the pack. Now stack yourself against each of the three winners: primary category, review count and recency, hours, photo freshness, NAP consistency. You will see the gap in about 10 minutes — it is almost never mysterious once you line up the fields side by side. One honest expectation: practitioners put realistic movement at 90 to 180 days, not a weekend. The pack is a compounding game — the businesses sitting in it started stacking reviews and profile completeness long before you noticed them there.
