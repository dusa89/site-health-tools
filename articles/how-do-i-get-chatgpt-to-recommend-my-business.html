How do I get ChatGPT to recommend my business?

A customer types “who's the best plumber near me?” into ChatGPT instead of Google. It answers in one confident paragraph, naming two businesses. One of them should have been you — and there was no ad to buy, no page-one to climb. Just a name the AI happened to know.

The stakes moved fast. The share of consumers using AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Mode to find local businesses jumped from 6 percent to 45 percent in a single year, making AI the number-three local discovery channel — ahead of Yelp and Tripadvisor (BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey, 2026, via Cheers). Over the same twelve months, Google's own share of local business discovery fell from 83 percent to 71 percent (a 2026 Digital Applied analysis of BrightLocal data). And the field is brutally thin: SOCi's Local Visibility Index, which analyzed more than 350,000 business locations, found that ChatGPT currently recommends just 1.2 percent of local businesses — roughly one in eighty — making an AI recommendation 3 to 30 times harder to earn than a spot in Google's 3-pack (SOCi, reported by MarketersMedia, 2026).

What ChatGPT actually reads about you

ChatGPT does not browse the web the way you do. When it answers a local question, it pulls from a fixed set of trusted sources. BrightLocal's 2026 local AI study ran tens of thousands of checks across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, and Google AI Overviews, and the citation map is public: for ChatGPT, Yelp is the top source. Your Google Business Profile was cited for roughly 94 percent of businesses, and Facebook for about 93 percent. Directories still matter — the study found 415 of them feeding these systems, including the Better Business Bureau, Angi, MapQuest, and industry-specific directories. Businesses with a fixed storefront address get named a bit more often than home-based service-area businesses (about 34 percent vs 29 percent of the time), because a public address is one more fact the AI can verify.

The good news hiding in those numbers: ChatGPT is not picking names out of thin air. It recommends businesses it can verify — the ones with consistent, detailed, review-backed profiles on the sources it trusts. That is a checklist, and most of it is free.

Six fixes, in order of leverage

1. Claim and finish your profiles on ChatGPT's three favorite sources. Yelp, Google Business Profile, Facebook — in that order of importance for ChatGPT specifically. Real categories, real services, real photos, real hours, and reply to every review: review text becomes attached to your name and is exactly the kind of detail the AI quotes. Free, and the single highest-leverage hour you can spend. (More on each: getting more reviews.)

2. Put a quotable answer at the top of every service page. The AI copies answers it can lift in one piece. Write a direct 40-to-60-word answer to the question customers actually ask — “who repairs water heaters in North Las Vegas?” — right at the top of the page, in plain sentences with your trade and your town named. Question-style headings and FAQ-style Q&A blocks on the page help too. You are writing for the AI's highlighter, not a poetry contest.

3. Make sure the AI's crawlers are allowed to read your site. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and their peers read websites with special crawlers (named things like GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot). Some websites accidentally block them in their robots.txt file — often from a broad “block all bots” rule their developer set years ago. If the crawler cannot read your page, the AI cannot cite it. Any web person can check this in minutes.

4. Give the AI a clean fact sheet. Behind the scenes, websites can carry structured business data — your name, trade, address, services, opening hours — written in a format the AI parses instantly, plus a simple /llms.txt file that points AI agents to your key pages. Think of it as a fact sheet in the AI's native language. Not a website redesign; your web person can add it in under an hour.

5. Get named places the AI reads. Mentions of your business name across the web — local news, industry directories, chamber listings, sponsor pages — are how the AI decides you are real and established. Do not chase backlinks for their own sake; chase being mentioned, consistently spelled, in places customers actually check.

6. Measure it monthly. Ask ChatGPT the same way a customer would — your trade, your town, plain words — once a month, and write down who gets named. These systems change slowly, so think in months, not days. If you are in the AI-visibility cluster already (Google's AI and why your competitor gets picked), you know the measurement drill.

The honest caveats

First: nobody can buy this the way they buy ads. There is no “top ChatGPT spot” for sale; the name has to be earned through verifiable information, which is also why this game is winnable for a small business with no marketing budget. Second: every AI product builds its own shortlist — getting named by ChatGPT does not mean Google's AI will name you too. Third: do not expect results this week. The systems re-scan sources slowly, so set your clock in months — and every fix above also strengthens your regular Google presence, so none of the work is wasted either way.
