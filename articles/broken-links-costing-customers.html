The dead links on your website are costing you customers

Somewhere on your site right now is a link that leads nowhere — a “Book appointment” button, a service page, a photo that never loads. Nobody told you, because visitors who hit a dead end don’t complain. They just don’t come back.

A broken link is simply a link that points somewhere that no longer exists. They creep in four ways, none of which feel like mistakes at the time: a page gets deleted and nobody updates the links pointing to it; a redesign changes your page addresses; someone typed the link wrong when the page was built; or an image got moved or renamed and the page still points at the old location. The damage shows up months later, one bounced visitor at a time.

It costs you twice

First, the human cost: a customer clicks “Book appointment,” lands on an error page, and calls your competitor instead — the one whose button worked. Second, the search-engine cost: Google’s crawlers trip over dead ends too, and a site full of them reads as unmaintained, which is not how you want to look to the thing that decides whether customers find you at all.

Why you’ve never noticed

Owners click their own sites far less than their customers do. You know where everything is, so you never take the path a first-time visitor takes — and the first-time visitor is exactly who finds the broken step. This is one of those problems that is invisible from the inside and obvious from the outside.
