Why are my emails suddenly going to spam?

Last month your quotes landed in inboxes. This month customers swear they never arrived. Nothing feels different on your end — but something changed underneath your email, and it is almost always one of four things.

When working email turns into spam-folder email overnight, owners blame the wrong thing first: the words in the message, the subject line, some imagined filter with a grudge. In our experience it is almost never the words. Spam-foldering that starts suddenly means something changed — a new tool, a broken record, a listing, or a rule that got stricter. Here are the four real causes, in the order we see them.

1. A new tool quietly broke your SPF record

This is the most common one. Your SPF record lists which services may send mail as you, and it has a hard ceiling: no more than 10 outside lookups. Every service you add — the newsletter tool, the CRM, the booking system — costs lookups. The record that worked for years breaks the week you add one more, SPF starts returning errors instead of passes, and inbox providers treat the errors like failures. Worse: nothing tells you. Email keeps “sending” fine from your side; it just stops arriving.

2. Your domain or mail server landed on a blocklist

Enough recipients marking your bulk mail as spam — or a spammer sharing your host’s mail server — can get your sending address listed. Free blocklist checkers will tell you which list you’re on in seconds. Getting off is usually a removal request plus a few days of clean sending after the underlying cause is fixed — which is why you check for cause #1 first.

3. Gmail and Yahoo tightened the rules for bulk senders

Since 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require anyone sending bulk mail — newsletters, coupon blasts, appointment reminders to a list — to authenticate properly and to offer one-click unsubscribe. These are enforced automatically, not announced to you personally. If your mailing-list mail suddenly started bouncing to spam while your one-to-one replies still land fine, this is the first place to look.

4. A service you forgot about is sending unsigned mail

The invoice tool, the review-request app, the old scheduler — any of them can send as your domain without your DKIM signature. Your DMARC record can include a reporting address, and the reports it collects are the detective’s file: they list exactly which services are failing authentication, by name. Most owners have never opened one; it usually names the culprit in the first page.
