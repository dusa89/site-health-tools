What does "website speed" actually mean?

When this check says your site takes 4.2 seconds, it means one thing: the moment your main content — the headline, the hero image, the reason the visitor came — becomes visible on a typical phone. Google calls this measurement Largest Contentful Paint. A score out of 100 sounds precise but tells you nothing about your business; seconds tell you whether a customer waits or leaves.

Google's published research found that more than half of mobile visitors abandon a page that takes longer than 3 seconds to load. That 2017 benchmark is still the industry's reference point, and phones have not gotten more patient since. Every extra second quietly costs you quotes, bookings, and sales — silently, because bounced visitors never complain.

The usual culprits are ordinary, not exotic: photos uploaded straight from a camera (often several times larger than any screen needs), plugins that each load their own code, chat widgets and trackers, and budget hosting that is slow to answer. None of this means your site is "bad" — Google's own homepage lands in the 50s on their 100-point scale. It means there is measurable, fixable waste, and this check names the three biggest pieces of it.
