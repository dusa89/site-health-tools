Does ChatGPT recommend your business?

Somewhere tonight, a customer typed “who is a good plumber near me?” — not into Google, but into ChatGPT. The AI named three businesses. Yours was not one of them, and you will never know it happened. This is the new front page of the internet, and most small businesses have never looked at it.

For twenty years, being found meant ranking on Google. Now a growing share of customers skip Google entirely and ask an AI assistant for a recommendation — “recommend a reliable HVAC company in Henderson,” “best family dentist near me.” The assistant answers in full sentences, names a few businesses, and the customer calls one of them. If you are never named, you are not losing a click — you are losing the whole conversation, silently.

How an AI assistant decides who to name

The honest version first: nobody outside the AI companies knows the exact recipe, and anyone who claims to is selling something. But the broad mechanics are public. An assistant answers from what it learned during training — a huge snapshot of the web — plus whatever current sources it is allowed to reach. When it names businesses, it favors the ones with clear, consistent public information: a website that says in plain words what the trade is and where it operates, listings that all use the same name and address, a claimed Google Business Profile, and reviews that mention the actual service. A business with a vague website, three different names across directories, and no reviews is hard for a human to verify — and it is hard for an AI too.

Why most free checkers do not actually answer the question

A wave of “AI visibility” tools launched this year. Independent testing found the free ones tend to hide the parts that matter: the actual question they asked, the verbatim answer, and which competitors got named — the good stuff sits behind a paywall, with real monitoring starting around $25 to $99 a month. A score without the receipts is entertainment. What you actually need to see is the answer itself, word for word, because that is exactly what your customer sees.

If you are not named, what moves the needle

The fixes are unglamorous, and that is good news. Spell out your trade and your service area in plain words on your homepage — not buried in a logo, written in a sentence. Make your business name, address, and phone number identical everywhere they appear. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile. Ask happy customers to review you and to mention the actual work (“fixed our AC” beats “great guy” for this purpose). None of this is fast — AI training data updates slowly, so think in months — but every one of these also helps your Google ranking today, so none of the work is wasted either way.
