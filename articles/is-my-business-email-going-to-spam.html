Is your business email going to spam?

A customer swears they never got your quote. You sent it; it never bounced. It went to spam — and nothing told you. Three invisible records decide whether that keeps happening, and most small businesses have never heard of them.

Think of SPF, DKIM, and DMARC as three stamps on every email you send. SPF is a public list, kept in your domain’s settings, naming which mail servers are allowed to send email for you. When Gmail receives a message claiming to be from your business, it checks the list. DKIM is a digital signature your mail server adds to each message — a wax seal proving nobody altered it in transit. DMARC is your written instruction to inbox providers: here’s what to do with mail that fails the first two checks, and where to send you the reports about it.

What goes wrong when they’re missing

Two bad things happen quietly. First, your real email looks more suspicious, so more of it lands in spam — the quotes, invoices, and appointment confirmations your customers never see and never tell you about. Second, anyone on earth can send email pretending to be your domain, and inbox providers have no instruction to stop it. Your customers get the scam; you get the blame.

The fix is smaller than you think

Each missing record is one small entry in your domain’s DNS settings — the same place your website’s address lives, usually your registrar like GoDaddy or Cloudflare.
