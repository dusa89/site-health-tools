Do online reviews decide what ChatGPT says about your business?

ChatGPT does not just read your website before recommending a business — it cross-examines your reputation across the whole web. The 2026 research says reviews are the separator, your website is the biggest single vote, and a near-perfect rating is just the price of entry.

Ask ChatGPT to recommend a plumber in your town and it will not just glance at your homepage and nod politely. It goes and asks around — checking Google reviews, Yelp, Reddit threads, your license board, and the “best plumbers in town” lists, before it names a single business. Picture a hiring panel where the candidate never meets most of the interviewers: your website gets a seat at that table, but the other votes belong to strangers. And the research says those stranger-votes are doing more of the deciding than owners expect.

The review-depth finding: 226 vs 164

In August 2026, researchers at Spearleaf tracked how ChatGPT’s AI answers recommend local businesses across 216 markets — two query forms, two waves, with every rating validated against same-day Yelp and Google snapshots (a 50-card hand audit matched 49 of 50). Their finding on reviews was the steadiest signal in the whole study: businesses still being shown a week later carried a median of 226 reviews, against 164 for those that dropped off. The median displayed rating was 4.9 for both groups — and across every city tier, the median minimum rating on a displayed panel was 4.8.

Read the mechanism that suggests: a near-perfect rating is the price of entry; review volume is the separator. Being great is assumed. Being provably, repeatedly great is what keeps you in the answer. As the study’s founder Joshua Albanese put it: “You can’t hold a rank in AI search. You can only be worth recommending every time it looks, and be everywhere it looks… A deep bench of reviews at a near-perfect rating kept getting a business picked back up, wave after wave, and that is the part an owner actually controls.”

Where AI gets its “everywhere” from

A second analysis — 33,827 citations recorded across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity answers over 266 real business scans, by MentionedOn — found that 86% of citations point at a business’s own website, with directories and review platforms combined at about 10%. Your website is the single biggest vote. The reviews are the corroboration: when AI does cite platforms, the list is short and stable — Yelp, Angi, Reddit, Thumbtack, ConsumerAffairs, the BBB. And Google is not the only rating that shows up: 17.4% of the resolved rating cards in the Spearleaf study displayed Yelp ratings rather than Google’s — 139 of 799 cards, every one validated against Yelp’s own listing.

Two more numbers that matter. A BrightLocal analysis of ChatGPT’s local citations broke down as 58% business websites, 27% business mentions, and 15% directories — and inside that “mentions” slice, Wikipedia alone accounted for 39%. And in a 34,960-prompt study (arXiv 2609.23162), brands with no citations of their own website and no mentions anywhere else were named in only 2.8% of relevant ChatGPT answers. No website, no mentions, no reviews: you are effectively invisible.

The honest caveat: AI mostly echoes Google — so far

Here is the part the “AI SEO” sales pitches leave out. In that same Spearleaf study, when ChatGPT’s AI Overview named businesses, 87.6% of them were already sitting in the same page’s local pack (127 markets). The AI is, to a large extent, restating Google’s local ranking with its own wording. And the biggest single separator was not reviews at all — it was having an address in the asked-about city, with a descriptive odds ratio of 14.4 (92.0% of displayed businesses had an in-city address vs 73.9% of non-displayed).

So the honest order of operations is: be a real local business with the right Google footprint first, and then reviews decide which of the qualified local businesses the AI keeps naming. Reviews do not override a missing or out-of-town presence. They settle the ties.
