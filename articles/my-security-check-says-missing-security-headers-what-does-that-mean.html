My Security Check Says I'm Missing 'Security Headers' - What Does That Mean?

Your padlock is on and your certificate is valid, but the security check flags "missing security headers" and it reads like engineer-speak. It is not. It means your server is forgetting to send each visitor's browser a short set of standing orders - invisible bodyguards that stand between your customers and real attacks.

All facts below are reported from the cited sources, checked 2026-10-13 - not promises about your situation. Every site is different; these are the standard protections, described in plain English.

The short answer

Every time your site loads, your server sends the visitor's browser your page plus a small bundle of instructions most people never see. Security headers are those instructions. They tell the browser: always use the encrypted connection, only load code from the approved list, never let another site put this page inside an invisible frame, do not guess what files are, and do not leak page addresses to other sites. A site can have a perfect padlock and still be missing all five. The padlock means the road between your customer and you is encrypted. The headers are the guards posted along it.

Guard one: HSTS - "never take the dirt road"

Strict-Transport-Security tells browsers to always use HTTPS for your site, for however long you set it - the standard target is one year (31,536,000 seconds). Without it, a visitor who types your address without the https, or clicks an old http link, starts on the unencrypted road - and on coffee-shop wifi, an attacker on the network can keep them there and read or rewrite the traffic. With it, the browser upgrades to the secure road automatically on every visit from then on. The setup ramps up: start with a short memory (five minutes) to verify nothing breaks, then raise it to a year. Adding includeSubDomains extends the order to every subdomain - but check first that every subdomain actually works over HTTPS, or you will lock yourself out of the ones that do not. The strongest version is preload: your domain goes on a list baked into Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge themselves, so even a first-time visitor on a brand-new device gets the secure road. Treat preload as a one-way door - getting off the list takes months.

Guard two: CSP - the guest list

Content-Security-Policy is a guest list for your page. It says exactly which places your pages may load scripts, images, and frames from. This is the main defense against the attack where a hacker slips a malicious script into your page - the same family of attack behind checkout skimmers, the invisible code that steals card numbers as customers type (our card-theft guide covers how those work). Without a guest list, the browser runs any code it finds. With one, injected code from an unapproved source is refused. The trap: a guest list that is too strict can block your own booking widget, embedded videos, or payment buttons. That is why a report-only mode exists - run the policy in watch mode for a week, read what would have been blocked, fix the list, then switch to enforce. Watch mode alone gives zero protection; it is the rehearsal, not the show.

Guard three: no invisible frames - the clickjacking rule

Clickjacking is a con: another site puts your page inside an invisible frame, layers its own buttons on top, and the visitor clicks what they see while actually clicking what is hidden - a "confirm payment" they never meant to approve. The guard against it says your page may never be framed, or only by your own site. The old guard is the X-Frame-Options header (DENY or SAMEORIGIN). The modern one is CSP's frame-ancestors directive, which does the same job with more flexibility - you can allow named partner sites instead of just all-or-nothing. Modern browsers use frame-ancestors when both are present; the old header is the backstop for old browsers, so the recommendation is to send both with matching meaning. The good news: a policy containing only frame-ancestors is valid and restricts nothing else - it is one of the easiest pieces of CSP to deploy.

Guards four and five: the quiet ones

X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff tells browsers not to second-guess what a file is. Without it, a browser can be talked into treating a disguised file as executable code - the classic way a harmless-looking upload turns hostile. One line, set everywhere. Referrer-Policy controls what your site tells other sites about where a visitor came from. The recommended minimum - strict-origin-when-cross-origin - sends only your domain name, not the full page address with its paths and query strings, to outside sites. One header most guides now say to skip: X-XSS-Protection. The old browser XSS filters it controlled have been removed, and in some cases they leaked information themselves - omit it or set it to 0.

The grade your scanner shows - and what it does not mean

Mozilla's free Observatory - the same engine behind our security check - scores sites from 0 to 100 and translates that into a letter grade from F to A+. The score is built from exactly these headers: CSP, HSTS, X-Frame-Options, X-Content-Type-Options, cookie flags, and a few more. Landing low is easy - the bar is honestly low in practice: even example.com grades around B/C with HSTS set but CSP and Permissions-Policy missing (per an October 2026 header-grading demo) - so a D on a small-business site is normal, not a crisis, but it does mean the guards are not posted. One honest caveat, straight from Mozilla: an A+ does not mean unhackable. The scan checks the configuration, not your actual code - it will not find an outdated plugin, a weak password, or a vulnerable contact form. The headers are the fence. The locks on the doors are a separate job (our padlock guide and Not Secure guide cover the other half).

The one ask for your web person

All five headers are configuration, not code - nobody is rewriting your website. Your host or web person adds them at the server (or in Cloudflare or your CDN's edge rules), typically a fifteen-to-thirty-minute job. Hand them this sentence: "Please add HSTS with a one-year max-age, a Content-Security-Policy with frame-ancestors set, X-Content-Type-Options set to nosniff, and a Referrer-Policy - and start the CSP in report-only mode first." If your site sits behind Cloudflare, it can be done as edge rules with no server changes at all. Then re-run the free check and watch the grade move.
