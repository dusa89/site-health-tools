Why is my homepage slow but the rest of my website is fine?

Six seconds on the homepage, two on an inner page, same server. The homepage is the junk drawer of the website — every video, slider, feed, and widget lives there. Here are the four culprits and the fix order.

Your homepage takes six seconds. A service page takes two. Same server, same theme, same hosting bill — and the page that matters most is the slowest one you own.

This is not a server problem. It is a weight problem, and the weight lives exactly where you put it: on the homepage. The homepage is the junk drawer of the website. Every widget, every feed, every video, every hero image the designer showed you — they all live on the page every visitor sees first. The inner pages carry the content. The homepage carries the decorations, and decorations weigh more than content.

Culprit one: the hero video

The single heaviest thing on most homepages is the background video — the drone shot, the office montage, the smiling-team loop. A single 30-second background video can easily weigh more than the entire rest of the page put together (illustration, not a stat — but pull up your own numbers and you will usually find it is directionally true). Videos start downloading the moment the page opens, they compete with everything else on the page for the connection, and on a phone they are the difference between a homepage and a loading screen.

The test: run our free speed check on your homepage, then run it on a plain inner page. The gap between the two numbers is the homepage's extra weight — not the server, not the host, just the page. If the homepage is twice as slow, the extra second or three is weight you chose to put there.

Culprit two: the slider

Sliders load every slide's image up front — all five of them — even though the visitor sees one. A slider with five unoptimized photos is five homepages' worth of hero image stacked behind one frame. And the honest data on sliders is grim: almost nobody clicks past the first slide. You are loading five billboards and showing one.

Culprit three: below-the-fold images that load anyway

The homepage keeps going: the gallery strip, the testimonial avatars, the logos of "trusted by" companies, the team photos. If those load the instant the page opens instead of when the visitor scrolls to them, the page spends its first seconds fetching images nobody has looked at yet. This is the single most common fix in the industry for a reason: the median mobile page now weighs 2.6 megabytes (3.0 MB on desktop, September 2026, via colorlib.com aggregating HTTP Archive data), images are the heaviest piece at about 0.9 MB on the median mobile page, and the median page makes 70 separate requests. Almost none of that needs to arrive in the first second.

Culprit four: every third-party widget firing on the first paint

The chat bubble, the review widget, the social-media feed, the booking calendar, the popup — the homepage collects integrations the way a fridge collects magnets. JavaScript is now what is driving page-weight growth: median mobile JavaScript rose from 358 KB in 2018 to 689 KB in September 2026 (same source), and much of that is third-party widgets the owner forgot they installed. Each one is a separate download, a separate connection, a separate chance to delay everything else.

Why it costs you more than a slow inner page

The homepage is the front door. Google's old-but-honest benchmark still frames the stakes: about 53% of mobile visitors abandon a page that takes longer than 3 seconds (Google, 2016 — dated, but the direction has only sharpened), and the bounce rate climbs 32% when load time goes from 1 second to 3 seconds (Google/SOASTA, 2017). A slow inner page costs you a reader. A slow homepage costs you the first impression, and there is no second page to recover on.

The homepage diet, in order

1. Kill or shrink the hero video. Either replace it with a single compressed poster image (a click-to-play button for the people who want it), or move it off the homepage. Nothing else on this page saves as many seconds.

2. Lazy-load everything below the fold. Gallery strips, avatars, logos, team photos — load them when the visitor scrolls to them, not before. Most modern site builders do this with a checkbox now; the photo guide covers the image half of this.

3. Delay the widgets. The chat bubble and the review feed do not need to load before the headline does. Load them after the page's first paint, or move the social feed to a subpage. (The plugin-and-widget guide shows how to audit what is actually running.)

4. Pick one call to action. Five buttons, three popups, and a newsletter form is not five times the conversion — it is five times the weight. One clear ask loads faster and converts better.

And if the whole site is slow — homepage and inner pages alike — that is a different diagnosis, and caching is the cheapest fix in the book. But when the gap is only on the homepage, the server was never the suspect. The junk drawer was.
