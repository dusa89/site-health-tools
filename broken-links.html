Where broken links come from (and why owners never notice)

A broken link is simply a link that points somewhere that no longer exists. They creep in four ways: a page gets deleted and nobody updates the links pointing to it; a redesign changes your page addresses; someone typed the link wrong when the page was built; or an image got moved or renamed and the page still points at the old location. None of these feel like mistakes at the time — the damage shows up months later, one bounced visitor at a time.

It costs you twice. First, the human cost: a customer clicks "Book appointment," hits an error page, and calls your competitor instead. Second, the search-engine cost: Google's crawlers trip over dead ends too, and a site full of them reads as unmaintained. You would never leave your shop's front door jammed — but most small-business sites are carrying dead links the owner has never seen, because owners click their own sites far less than their customers do.
